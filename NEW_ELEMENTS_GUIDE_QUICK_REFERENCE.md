## 1. Cost calculators (6 new)

You already had calculators for materials like cement, bricks and paint. These new ones cover **labour, pricing and everyday conversions**. Every rate they start with is an example figure that you can change to your own.

### Tiling Labour Calculator
- **What it is:** Works out how many days and how much money a tiling job needs in labour.
- **What it is for:** Quoting and planning tile-laying work.
- **How it helps:** Enter the area, how much one mason lays in a day, how many masons and helpers you have, and their daily wages. You get the number of days, the labour cost, and the cost per square metre. Skirting can be added.

### False Ceiling Calculator
- **What it is:** Estimates materials and cost for a false ceiling.
- **What it is for:** Quoting ceiling work.
- **How it helps:** Choose gypsum board, grid tiles or PVC panels, enter the area and wastage. It tells you how many boards or tiles, how much channel, how many screws and the total cost, including the cove or drop edges.

### Plumbing Pipe Calculator
- **What it is:** Works out pipes, fittings and cost for a plumbing run.
- **What it is for:** Ordering the right amount of pipe without guessing.
- **How it helps:** Enter the total run, the pipe size and material. You get the number of pipe lengths, how many fittings (elbows, tees), and the cost.

### Unit Converter
- **What it is:** Converts between the units people actually use on site: sq ft ↔ sq m, cu ft ↔ cu m, acre, cent, guntha, feet, metres, quintal, tonne and more.
- **What it is for:** Stopping unit mix-ups between drawings, suppliers and clients.
- **How it helps:** Type a number, pick the units, and it also shows the value in every other unit of that kind.

### Labour Productivity Calculator
- **What it is:** Shows how long a job will take and what the labour will cost.
- **What it is for:** Planning crews and checking whether a quote covers the labour.
- **How it helps:** Pick the kind of work (brickwork, plastering, painting and so on), enter the quantity and the crew. It gives the number of days, the total cost and the **cost per unit of work**.

### GST & Margin Calculator
- **What it is:** Prices a job from your cost, or splits an amount that already includes GST.
- **What it is for:** Setting a selling price with the profit you want, and showing the tax correctly on a quote.
- **How it helps:** Three modes: add a markup, aim for a target profit margin, or take a GST-inclusive amount apart. It shows profit, CGST and SGST (or IGST), and the invoice total. It treats *markup* and *margin* differently, so you don't under-price by mistake.

## 2. Everyday basics (10 new)

General-purpose building blocks that work for any kind of project.

### Signature Pad
- **What it is:** A box where a person signs with a mouse, finger or stylus.
- **What it is for:** Client or site sign-off, right on the canvas.
- **How it helps:** The signer's name, role and the date and time sit with the signature, so there is a clear record of who agreed and when. Clear it and sign again if needed.

### Photo Grid
- **What it is:** Up to six photos with a caption for each.
- **What it is for:** Showing site progress, deliveries or samples in one tidy place.
- **How it helps:** Photos live next to the work they belong to instead of in someone's phone or a chat group.

### Before / After
- **What it is:** Two photos side by side, labelled Before and After.
- **What it is for:** Showing the result of work done.
- **How it helps:** A clear way to prove progress to a client, or to document a repair.

### Location Pin
- **What it is:** A map with an address and coordinates, with an "Open in Maps" link.
- **What it is for:** Recording exactly where a site, shop or delivery point is.
- **How it helps:** No more "where is the site?" messages. A tap on the link opens directions. "Use my location" fills the coordinates from your phone.

### Rating
- **What it is:** A row of stars (3, 5 or 10) you click to set.
- **What it is for:** Scoring quality, a supplier, a finish or client satisfaction.
- **How it helps:** A quick, visible score that everyone can see at a glance.

### Date Range
- **What it is:** A start date and an end date, with the number of days between them.
- **What it is for:** Showing a work period, a booking or a contract term.
- **How it helps:** It tells you the state at a glance: "Starts in 5 days", "3 days left", "Last day" or "Ended". It warns you if the end date is before the start.

### Number with Unit
- **What it is:** One important number shown large, with its unit (for example 1,20,000 m²).
- **What it is for:** Highlighting a key figure: area, weight, quantity, capacity.
- **How it helps:** Important numbers stop getting lost inside paragraphs of text.

### QR Code
- **What it is:** A scannable code for a link or any short text.
- **What it is for:** Putting a document, a web page or a reference on a printed sheet or a label.
- **How it helps:** Anyone can scan it with a phone to open the item, so printed papers stay connected to digital information.

### Barcode
- **What it is:** A standard barcode for a reference number (up to 40 characters).
- **What it is for:** Tagging items, orders or files with a scannable reference.
- **How it helps:** Speeds up checking in stock and matching paperwork to goods.

### Callout
- **What it is:** A highlighted note in one of four styles: Info, Warning, Success or Critical.
- **What it is for:** Making sure an important message is not missed.
- **How it helps:** A "Do not load the slab for 48 hours" note stands out from ordinary text and is announced clearly to everyone who opens the canvas.

## 3. Printing and signage (5 new)

For vendors who make boards, banners, prints and packaging artwork. This is where jobs most often go wrong, through unclear feedback and unapproved changes.

### Proof Markup
- **What it is:** Artwork with numbered pins dropped on it. Each pin has a comment and a status: Open, Fixed or Approved.
- **What it is for:** Giving precise feedback on a design. "Pin 3: logo too small" is much clearer than a long message.
- **How it helps:**
  - Pins can be **dragged** to the right spot.
  - Pin numbers **never change**, even if another pin is deleted, so "see pin 3" always means the same thing.
  - A summary reads "2 open · 1 fixed · 4 approved", so you know how close the proof is to done.

### Artwork Approval Card
- **What it is:** The artwork, its version number (v1, v2, v3), and the client's decision.
- **What it is for:** Getting a clear, recorded "yes" before anything is printed.
- **How it helps:**
  - You choose who approves. A vendor asks the PM (or a CAS member), and the PM forwards it to the client. Everyone sees each approval, stamped with name and time (see "How approvals work").
  - **A new version always starts as "not sent".** An old approval can never be mistaken for approval of changed artwork.
  - Earlier versions stay in a list, so there is a history if a dispute comes up.

### Signage Elevation
- **What it is:** A drawing of a sign board to scale, with width, height and the height it is mounted at.
- **What it is for:** Showing a client and the installation team exactly what is being made and where it goes.
- **How it helps:** Type the sizes and the drawing follows, with the dimensions marked and the board's area shown. Material, frame, lighting and finish are listed beneath it.

### Colour Palette
- **What it is:** A set of colour swatches, each with a name and its HEX, CMYK and Pantone values.
- **What it is for:** Keeping brand colours consistent from design to print.
- **How it helps:** Each colour has a "Matched" tick, and the palette tells you "3 of 5 matched". A value that can't be read is outlined in red so typing errors are caught.

### Material Swatch Board
- **What it is:** Cards for materials (vinyl, ACP, acrylic, paper and so on) with a photo, finish, thickness and supplier.
- **What it is for:** A reference sheet to show clients their material options.
- **How it helps:** Material choices are agreed from a single page instead of scattered samples and messages.

## 4. Food and FMCG (5 new)

For food makers and packaged-goods suppliers, where records matter for quality and safety.

### Batch Record
- **What it is:** The record of one production batch: product, batch number, date, ingredients with their lot numbers, planned and actual output, and the quality check.
- **What it is for:** Having a clear history of exactly what went into each batch.
- **How it helps:**
  - It shows the **yield**: green when on target, amber when slightly under, red when well under.
  - You choose who approves it (the PM or a CAS member, for a vendor). It is an internal quality step, so it closes there and is not sent on to the client. A failed batch needs a reason.
  - **Once approved, the record is locked.** It can be reopened for a correction, and the earlier decision is kept on file, so an approved record can't be quietly changed.

### Expiry & Shelf-Life Tracker
- **What it is:** A list of items with their made-on and expiry dates.
- **What it is for:** Not missing the date on stock.
- **How it helps:** Items are sorted with the soonest expiry first, and each is marked green, amber or red with plain words ("15 days left", "Expired 2 days ago"). A bar shows how much of its shelf life is used.

### Temperature Log
- **What it is:** A log of readings for a cold room, freezer or storage area, against the allowed range.
- **What it is for:** Proving that food was kept at a safe temperature.
- **How it helps:** Each reading is marked In range, Too cold or Too warm. A small chart shows the trend with the safe band shaded, and a count shows how many readings went out of range. It works for freezers too (negative temperatures). A full log asks you to start a new one rather than quietly deleting old readings.

### FSSAI / Licence Tracker
- **What it is:** A list of licences and registrations with number, issuing authority, and expiry.
- **What it is for:** Never letting a licence lapse.
- **How it helps:** Each licence shows "Valid", "Renew in 20 days" or "Expired 39 days ago", with a summary at the top.

### Recall Trace Sheet
- **What it is:** For one batch, the raw materials used and everyone it was sent to.
- **What it is for:** Acting quickly and accurately if a product has to be pulled back.
- **How it helps:** It totals how much went out against how much was made, counts the customers, and warns if more was dispatched than produced. **Copy trace** puts a ready-to-send summary on your clipboard for a regulator, a customer or a recall team.

## 5. Interiors and furniture (5 new)

For interior designers, fit-out contractors and furniture makers, where many rooms, many finishes and many decisions have to stay in step.

### Room-wise Scope Sheet
- **What it is:** Each room with the work items inside it, their finish, and their status (To do, In progress, Done, On hold). Each room folds open and closed.
- **What it is for:** Agreeing and tracking exactly what is being done in every room.
- **How it helps:** Every room shows "3 of 5 done" and the whole sheet shows an overall percentage, so a client or site supervisor can see where the project stands in a few seconds. Finished rooms are marked Complete.

### Finish Schedule
- **What it is:** A grid with the rooms down the side and the surfaces (floor, wall, ceiling, and any you add) across the top. Each box holds a material and a code.
- **What it is for:** One reference for what goes where, which the whole team and every supplier can work from.
- **How it helps:** It lists where each code is used. **If the same code has been given to two different materials, it warns you,** which catches the kind of mistake that usually shows up on site.

### Furniture Spec Card
- **What it is:** A card for one piece of furniture with a photo, name, size, material, finish, quantity and supplier.
- **What it is for:** Giving a carpenter or supplier a complete, unambiguous brief.
- **How it helps:** All the details sit with the picture, so the same question is not asked three times. Sizes can be in mm, cm, inches or feet, and quantity is always a whole number.

### Moodboard
- **What it is:** A free board where you place pictures and colour swatches, add captions, and ask the client to approve.
- **What it is for:** Agreeing the look and feel before any money is spent.
- **How it helps:**
  - Tiles are **dragged** wherever you like, and can't be dragged off the board.
  - You choose who approves. A vendor asks the PM, and the PM forwards it to the client. Everyone sees each approval, stamped with name and time.
  - **Once approved, the board is locked** so it can't drift. It can be reopened, and the earlier approval is kept on record.

### Site Measurement Sheet
- **What it is:** Notes from measuring a room: each wall's length and height, the doors and windows, and a note (with an optional photo of your hand sketch).
- **What it is for:** Taking measurements once, properly, on the site visit.
- **How it helps:** It works out the **total wall length, the wall area, the area taken up by openings, and the net wall area** for you. A wall with no height uses the ceiling height. Anything it can't work out is listed as not counted, instead of being guessed, and it warns you if the openings look larger than the walls.

## 6. Construction (6 new)

For contractors and site teams. These are the **records that fill up day after day**, with a row for every defect, check, cube or item.

They sit alongside the Site Work Order, RFI, Inspection Request and Daily Site Log you already have. Those are forms you raise and send. These are working sheets where the lines get filled in on site.

### Snag / Punch List
- **What it is:** A numbered list of defects. Each has a location, a description, an owner, a due date and an optional photo.
- **What it is for:** Getting a building or a room ready for handover.
- **How it helps:**
  - Each snag moves from **Open** to **Fixed** to **Verified**. A snag is only closed when someone named checks it.
  - Overdue snags show first, in red. A summary reads "12 snags · 7 verified · 3 fixed, awaiting check · 2 open · 1 overdue", with the percentage cleared.
  - **Snag numbers never change.** If snag 4 is deleted, snag 5 stays snag 5, so "see snag 5" is always true.

### Inspection Checklist
- **What it is:** A list of checks for one stage of work (pre-pour, reinforcement, waterproofing and so on). Each line is marked Pass, Fail or N/A, with a remark.
- **What it is for:** Recording the **result** of an inspection. The Inspection Request asks for an inspection to happen, and this records what was found.
- **How it helps:**
  - Choose a stage and the usual checks load for you. Edit, remove or add your own.
  - **It can only be accepted when every line has passed (or is N/A).** A pending or failed line blocks it and tells you how many.
  - You choose who approves. A vendor asks the PM (or a CAS member), and the PM forwards it to the client. Each stamp is visible to everyone, and a rejection needs a reason.
  - While it is with an approver, and once approved, the lines are locked until it is reopened.

### Concrete Pour Card
- **What it is:** The details of one pour (where, which member, grade, volume, slump) and the cube test results.
- **What it is for:** Keeping the strength results next to the pour they belong to.
- **How it helps:**
  - Enter the grade (M25, for example). Each cube is marked **Meets**, **Below** or **Not tested**, with the average for 7 and 28 days.
  - It works out **when each test is due** from the pour date, and tells you when a result is overdue.
  - The check is **indicative**, against the grade. Final acceptance follows your project specification.

### Measurement Sheet
- **What it is:** The measurement book as a sheet: rows of number × length × breadth × depth, with deductions.
- **What it is for:** Working out a quantity you can defend, for example before a bill.
- **How it helps:**
  - A blank length, breadth or depth is simply left out, as in a measurement book. Deductions go on their own rows, so there are no negative numbers to get wrong.
  - It shows additions, deductions and the **net quantity**. A row with a number it can't use is flagged and left out, rather than guessed.
  - A gentle hint appears if the dimensions don't suit the unit (for example, m³ with only two dimensions).

### Running Bill Tracker
- **What it is:** A running account bill: items with the BOQ quantity, rate, previous quantity and this bill's quantity.
- **What it is for:** Seeing how much is being billed now, and how far each item has progressed.
- **How it helps:**
  - For every item: the quantity to date, the percentage complete, and the amount for this bill.
  - **An item above its BOQ quantity is flagged** in amber.
  - It works out gross, GST, retention, advance recovery and **net payable**. A fold-out explains the sum in plain words.
  - It is a simplified view. Check it against your contract terms.

### Permit to Work
- **What it is:** A permit for risky work (hot work, working at height, excavation, confined space, electrical isolation, lifting), with its precautions, validity window, **approval**, and close-out.
- **What it is for:** Making sure the safety steps are confirmed and **someone accountable approves** before the work starts, and that the permit is closed afterwards.
- **How it helps:**
  - **Issued by** fills itself in with the person who dropped the element. Nobody types it, so it can't be changed.
  - You **choose who approves** from the people you are allowed to ask (see "How approvals work"). The request appears on the shared canvas in the same task and subtask, and that person is notified.
  - It can only be sent when every precaution is answered with no "No" left, and the end time is still ahead.
  - Each approver's name and the date and time are stamped on the permit and visible to everyone.
  - Once approved it shows **Valid**, **Expires within the hour**, or **Expired: stop work and close**. Closing needs a name and a tick that the area has been made safe. Cancelling needs a reason.

## How approvals work

Five elements need a sign-off: the **Permit to Work**, **Inspection Checklist**, **Artwork Approval Card**, **Moodboard** and **Batch Record**. They all use the same rules.

**Who can be asked, and what happens next**

| Who sends it | They can ask | Then |
|---|---|---|
| **Vendor** | The **PM** or a **CAS member**. Never the client directly. | The PM forwards it to the **client**, and it closes when the client approves. If a CAS member was asked first: CAS → PM → client. |
| **CAS member** | The **PM** | PM → client |
| **Client** | The **PM** | The PM forwards it to a **vendor**, and it closes when the vendor approves. |
| **PM** | Anyone: vendors, the client, CAS members | That person approves and it closes. |

- **If there is no client** on the project, the PM's approval closes it. The same goes for the batch record, which is an internal quality step and closes with the PM or CAS member.
- **Choosing who:** if there is only one person to ask, they are chosen for you. If there are several, you get a dropdown. When the PM forwards, the same applies.
- **Sent back:** anyone in the chain can send it back with a reason. It returns to the person who sent it, who fixes it and sends it again.
- **Everyone sees everything.** The element and every stamp on it (who approved, and when) is visible to all members on the canvas. A vendor sees the client's stamp.
- **While it is with an approver, and once it is approved, it is locked.** Reopen it to change it.
- **These rules are enforced on the server,** not only on the screen. A vendor cannot ask the client directly, and an approval cannot be forged or edited.

## Good to know

- **All rates and limits can be changed.** Calculator rates, warning periods (for example "warn 30 days before expiry") and yield targets are sensible starting points, not rules. Set them to match your business.
- **Approvals are stamped with the approver's name and the time,** taken from their signed-in account. They are a clear working record, but they are not a legal e-signature.
- **Uploads need a subtask.** Photos, artwork, snag photos and sketches are saved inside a subtask, so open one before uploading.
- **People with view-only access can see everything but can't change anything.** They can't sign, approve, tick, log or upload.
- **If two people edit the same element at the same moment,** the most recent save wins. It's best to let one person update a record at a time.
- **Food and site records are working records, not certified systems.** They are a good, tidy record, but they don't replace any audit, statutory permit format or code-based acceptance your regulator or contract requires.

## Where to start

If you only try a few, these give the most value quickly:

1. **Proof Markup** and **Artwork Approval Card**: stops printing mistakes and "I never approved that" disputes.
2. **Signature Pad** and **Photo Grid**: proof of work and sign-off in one place.
3. **GST & Margin Calculator**: price a job correctly in a minute.
4. **Expiry & Shelf-Life Tracker** and **Licence Tracker**: nothing lapses unnoticed.
5. **Site Measurement Sheet** and **Moodboard**: measure once, and get the look approved before work starts.
6. **Snag List**, **Inspection Checklist** and **Permit to Work**: the records a site is asked to show, kept in one place.
